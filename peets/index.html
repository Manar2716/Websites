01 — The roast

DEEP ROASTED

Taken further than most dare to and pulled the moment the oils come up. It is the darker, richer cup Alfred Peet opened with on Walnut Street, and it has not been lightened since.

02 — The cup

HANDCRAFTED

No automatic bar, no push-button shot. Every drink on this menu is pulled, steamed and finished by a barista standing in front of you.

03 — Extraction

TWENTY-FIVE SECONDS

Nine bars at ninety-three degrees through eighteen grams. The crema comes up tiger-striped and holds for four minutes.

04 — The pour

SIXTY-TWO DEGREES

And not one degree past it. Milk taken further stops tasting sweet and starts tasting cooked. The rosetta is drawn in one pass and pulled through at the end.

06 — The bar

MADE TO ORDER

A lever group, a copper boiler and a burr set trued to two microns — the same bench in every store, so the cup in Berkeley is the cup in Boston.

NOTHING IS RUSHED

Beans roasted to order and shipped within days. Nothing sits, nothing is stretched, nothing is hidden under a flavour.

Since 1966

The craft

A Dutchman who had grown up in his father's roastery opened a shop on a corner in Berkeley and started roasting darker than anyone in the country. People queued for it. Most of what American coffee became afterwards started in that queue.

  1. 1966

    Walnut and Vine

    One shop, one roaster, and beans sold whole so nobody could stale them on the way home. The queue on the pavement is still the shape of the business.

  2. 1971

    The apprentices

    Three of them learned the roast here and left to open their own shop in Seattle. That is the whole story of American speciality coffee in one sentence.

  3. 1984

    Roast to order

    Beans go into the drum against the orders on the board, not against a forecast. It costs more and it is the only way freshness means anything.

  4. Today

    Still by hand

    Every roast is signed off by a person watching colour and listening for the crack. There is a machine that could do it. It does not do it as well.

The film, in words

One bean

A roasted seed is an ellipsoid with one flat face and a crease down the middle of it. Under a single hard light that crease catches the highlight and loses it again once per turn, and that is the whole first shot.

Deep roasted

The roast is taken further than most and pulled the moment the oils come up on the surface of the bean. A minute past that is char; a minute short of it is the flat, sour cup the country drank before 1966.

Handcrafted

No automatic bar and no push-button shot. A barista grinds, doses, tamps, pulls and finishes every drink, which is slower, and is the reason two people ordering the same latte get the same latte.

Twenty-five seconds

Nine bars at ninety-three degrees through eighteen grams. The crema arrives tiger-striped, holds for about four minutes, and is the first thing to tell you whether the grind was right.

Sixty-two degrees

Milk is steamed to sixty-two and not one degree past it. Beyond that the lactose stops reading as sweetness and the cup turns flat and slightly cooked.

Made to order

A lever group, a copper boiler and a burr set trued to two microns. The same bench in every store, maintained on the same schedule, so the cup does not depend on which door you walked through.
