SOURCING

WHERE IT COMES FROM

Four things decide whether a bucket is any good, and three of them are settled before the kitchen touches it.

01 — THE FOUR

NOTHING IS FROZEN TWICE

Gulf prawn

Landed at Deira and on ice within the hour. Head on, always — the head is where the flavour of the boil actually comes from, and a peeled prawn gives the stock nothing.

Snow crab

Cold water, flown in, cooked once at sea and once here. The second cook is short, and it is the only one that matters. Anything longer and the meat goes to string.

The butter

Cultured, clarified, then rebuilt with shell stock and thirty-four spices. It is not a sauce and it is not a dip. It is the dish, and the seafood is what carries it.

The heat

Four levels. Cayenne, two chillies and a long pepper that arrives late and stays. The top one is not a joke and the staff will ask you twice.

02 — THE DAY

ELEVEN HOURS

  1. 04:30

    Deira

    The prawn is bought whole off the quay. What is not good enough that morning is simply not on the menu that night.

  2. 07:00

    The shells

    Yesterday's shells go into water with fennel, bay and a great deal of black pepper. They will not come out for six hours.

  3. 10:00

    The blend

    Thirty-four spices, toasted the night before, ground and folded into clarified butter with garlic that has been cooking since eight.

  4. 13:00

    The stock

    Strained, reduced by two thirds, and held. Every bucket for the next twelve hours comes out of this pot.

  5. 18:00

    Service

    Baskets go in to order. Twenty-five minutes, one pail at a time, until the stock is gone — which is usually somewhere around midnight.
