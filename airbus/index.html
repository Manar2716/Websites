02 — Anatomy

Read it
like a drawing

The outline below draws itself as you scroll, stroke by stroke, in proportion to each path's real length. Then five things worth knowing arrive in the order an engineer would introduce them.

01 · Flight deck

No cables to anything

Sidestick inputs are requests, not commands. Flight-control computers decide what the surfaces actually do, and refuse anything outside the envelope.

02 · Fuselage

A pressure vessel first

Everything else is negotiable; the circular section is not. Composite skins let the cabin hold a lower altitude and more humidity without paying for it in fatigue.

03 · Wing

It changes shape in flight

An adaptive trailing edge trims camber continuously as fuel burns off, so the wing keeps flying its best shape at a weight that drops by a third over a long sector.

04 · Engines

Mostly bypass

The great majority of the thrust never touches the combustor. A high bypass ratio moves more air more slowly — quieter, and far cheaper per kilometre.

05 · Empennage

Trim is fuel

Moving fuel aft into the tail shifts the centre of gravity rearward, unloading the tailplane. Less download to carry means less induced drag for the whole cruise.

03 — Performance

Payload is a loan
against fuel

An aircraft has one maximum take-off weight and two ways to spend it. The curve below is where that argument is settled: flat while structure limits you, sloping the moment fuel starts buying range at payload's expense.

Payload–range envelope · A350-1000

Payload (t) × range (km)

Hover the curve to read any point. The two corners are the limits that actually bind: maximum structural payload, then maximum usable fuel. Curve shape is modelled from each type's published range and weights and is illustrative, not a loading manual.

04 — Network

Range is a shape,
not a number

On a sphere, "8 700 nautical miles" is a region, and the shortest track between two cities is almost never the one a flat map draws. Every arc below is slerped between real coordinates; every distance is computed, not typed. Drag the globe.

Drag to rotate · select a sector

Reachable by A350-1000

Within range Beyond range

Sectors are gated against the selected type's published range — change the aircraft in the fleet section and the network genuinely shrinks. Block times assume cruise Mach at FL350 plus a fixed 25-minute ground-and-terminal allowance.

05 — Cabin

Light is the only
thing that moves

Nothing in a cabin can change on a long sector except the air and the light. A lighting programme walks the cabin through a day it is not actually having — which is the point, when you have crossed eight time zones sitting still.

Colophon

Built out of
maths, not media

No image files, no fonts fetched, no libraries, no network calls. Every aircraft is generated from a parameter set, the sky is a few hundred pre-rendered gradients blitted twice a frame, and the globe is orthographic projection with spherical interpolation. One requestAnimationFrame loop drives all of it.
