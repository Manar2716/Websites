HOW IT WORKS

Four things
happen at once.

The housing comes off as you scroll. Everything below is what's under it.

  1. STEP 01 TWO MOTORS MOVE THE HEAD CoreXY: both motors are bolted to the frame and drive one continuous belt loop. Turn them together and the head goes left; turn them opposite and it goes forward. Because neither motor rides along, there is almost nothing to accelerate — that's where 20 000 mm/s² comes from.
  2. STEP 02 THE FILAMENT MELTS A gear pulls 1.75 mm filament into an all-metal hotend held at up to 300 °C. It leaves the hardened nozzle as a bead about 0.4 mm across and fuses into the layer below while it's still soft. That fusing is the whole part's strength.
  3. STEP 03 THE PLATE DROPS 0.08 mm When a layer closes, three lead screws lower the build plate by exactly one layer height and the head starts the next outline. The part never moves up — the floor moves down, 480 times.
  4. STEP 04 IT CHECKS ITS OWN WORK A lidar module reads the plate at 7 µm before anything is laid, then reads back the first layer it printed. If the bead is thin or lifting, it re-lays it — before the second layer commits the mistake to the whole part.

WHAT IT'S MADE OF

Six materials.
All of them nameable.

Nothing in this machine is a mystery composite. If a part wears out you can identify it, buy it, and fit it with hex keys — which is the only honest definition of repairable.

  • EXTRUDED ALUMINIUM Frame · 94% post-industrial

    Four uprights and eight cross members in 2020 profile. The whole chassis is bolted, never welded, so a bent member is a ten-minute swap rather than a write-off.

  • HARDENED STEEL Linear rails · nozzle · lead screws

    Ground rails on all three axes instead of wheels on extrusion. They cost more and they are the reason the machine still holds tolerance after a thousand hours.

  • BOROSILICATE GLASS Door · lid · 4 mm tempered

    Holds the chamber at 60 °C without hazing or bowing, and lets you watch the thing work. A printer you can't see into is a printer you stop trusting.

  • RECLAIMED BAMBOO PLY Side panels · 6 mm laminate

    Offcuts from flooring production, pressed into panels. Bamboo reaches structural density in five years where hardwood needs sixty.

  • PEI ON SPRING STEEL Build plate · textured both faces

    Parts stick when the plate is hot and release when it isn't. Flex it once and the print pops off — no scraper, no chisel, no gouged bed.

  • ACTIVATED CARBON + HEPA Filtration · replaceable cartridge

    Catches the ultrafine particles and the styrene smell before they reach the room. The cartridge is a consumable, and it is the only one.

WHAT IT EATS

Four spools.
All of them compost or return.

  • BAMBOO PLA 210–230°C · 1.75mm

    Culm flour in a PLA matrix. Matte, warm, and it will break down in an industrial digester in 90 days.

  • RECYCLED PETG 240–255°C · 1.75mm

    Reground from our own failed prints and bottle stock. Tough, slightly translucent, endlessly re-runnable.

  • PINE RESIN CO-PE 225–245°C · 1.75mm

    Rosin-toughened copolymer. Smells like the reference photo. Layer adhesion you can't tear by hand.

  • HEMP CARBON 260–285°C · 1.75mm

    Bast fibre standing in for half the carbon. Stiff, dimensionally quiet, needs the hardened nozzle.
